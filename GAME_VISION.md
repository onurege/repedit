# GAME VISION — BUSINESS DISTRICT

*(Long-term direction. Only Phase 1 is implemented — see CURRENT_PHASE.md.)*

## The dream

A persistent, shared, miniature 3D city where every business you can see is
owned and operated by a real player. Players start with a single small
business, master its production loop, and grow — first into a bigger building,
then into supply relationships with other players, and eventually into
multi-company business empires that shape the city's economy.

The feeling we are chasing:

> "I can see another player's business in the same city, buy something their
> business actually produced, watch that product travel to my business, use it
> in my own economy, and continue growing."

## Pillars

1. **Visible economy.** Goods are real: produced, stored, moved by vehicles you
   can watch, consumed. No abstract numbers pretending to be trade.
2. **Players need each other.** Supply chains span players. NPCs set price
   floors/ceilings and guarantee liquidity, but the interesting margins come
   from player-to-player deals.
3. **A city worth looking at.** Premium low-poly miniature aesthetic — soft
   light, saturated palette, readable buildings that visibly grow with success.
4. **Respect for time.** Businesses run while you're away (within limits).
   Sessions are satisfying at 10 minutes and at 2 hours.
5. **Server truth.** The simulation is authoritative and fair. Clients render
   and request; they never decide.

## Where it goes (future phases, not implemented)

- More industries: bakeries, restaurants, mini-markets, factories — deeper
  production chains (grain → flour → pastry).
- Employees and managers who automate routine decisions.
- Player logistics companies that compete to move goods.
- Contracts: recurring supply agreements between players with reputational
  stakes.
- Banking, loans, real estate, holding companies, multiple businesses per
  player.
- A living city: day/night, weather, traffic, districts with land value.
- Multi-city worlds and inter-city trade.

The architecture keeps definitions data-driven (products, business types,
upgrade levels) so these can be added without rewrites — but no future-phase
system is built before its time.
